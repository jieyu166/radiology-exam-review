## 詳解附圖（OCR）
**TEACHING POINTS**
- Radiologists encounter two main types of CA in clinical settings: immunoglobulin light-chain amyloidosis (AL) and transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTR). ATTR can be subdivided into two types according to genetic mutation status: hereditary or mutant type (ATTRm) and wild type (ATTRwt).
- Radiologists should be familiar with the following key clinical points regarding CA: (a) Many patients with heart failure, particularly those with HFpEF, also have "hidden" CA. (b) Most patients with CA remain undiagnosed and do not receive appropriate treatment. (c) CA generally is associated with a poor prognosis, especially for patients with advanced heart failure. (d) Early diagnosis and an intervention based on effective therapies are important factors in prognostic improvement. (e) Advanced diagnostic imaging can facilitate the early diagnosis of CA.

- The important cardiac manifestations of CA include increased left ventricular (LV) thickening, HFpEF, atrial fibrillation, bundle branch block, and complete heart block. Extracardiac symptoms that should also increase suspicion regarding CA include bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome, spinal canal stenosis, rotator cuff tear, peripheral and autonomic neuropathy, proteinuria, chronic diarrhea, weight loss, macroglossia, periorbital purpura, and jaw claudication.
- In CA, the typical LGE pattern involves global subendocardial LGE distributed in a non–coronary artery territory with a dark blood pool.
- When differentiating patients with ATTR (including ATTRm and ATTRwt) from those with AL or nonamyloid heart failure, the cardiac uptake of radiotracers such as technetium 99m (99mTc) pyrophosphate (99mTc-PYP), 99mTc–3,3-diphosphono-1,2-propanodicarboxylic acid (99mTc-DPD), and 99mTc–hydroxymethylene diphosphonate at bone scintigraphy has high diagnostic performance.
