#交換 #2021交換 #MSK All of the following are characteristic features of synovial sarcomas of the hand and wrist, except
(A) When small, T2 signal equal to fluid on MRI
(B) Mass may have calcification on radiography
(C) When large, “bowl of grapes” appearance on MRI
(D) Circumscribed, nonspecific mass on ultrasound
(E) Presentation as a painless nodule
??
**Ans: A**
【2021 官方詳解】
Synovial sarcoma generally presents at 15 to 40 years of age as a painless nodule. Although tumor growth may be slow, these lesions have high metastatic potential; 30% have calcifications, which may be visible on radiography. A circumscribed, nonspecifi c mass is typical on ultrasound. When the lesion is small, MRI shows a homogeneous, T1 intermediate and T2 hyperintense mass. T2 signal is not quite fluid bright, and special care should be taken not to mischaracterize a small synovial sarcoma as a benign ganglionic cyst. When lesions are larger, MRI signal is heterogeneous with septations, hemorrhage, and fluid (“bowl of grapes” appearance).
