## 詳解附圖（OCR）
**TEACHING POINTS**

- The risk of NSF after GBCA exposure depends on which agent is used, with most cases reported after use of nonionic linear agents.
- Administration of higher than standard doses (>0.1 mmol/kg) of GBCAs has been associated with development of NSF.
- The dramatic reduction of new cases of NSF over the last decade may have occurred because of more routine screening for kidney disease, less use of the GBCAs that have been associated with the most cases of NSF (particularly in patients with renal dysfunction), and less use of the high-dose GBCA-enhanced examinations that were sometimes used in MR angiography.
- To our knowledge, there have been no studies in animals or humans demonstrating clinical, behavioral, or biologic changes associated with neuronal gadolinium deposition.
- Neuronal GBCA deposition has been reported after administration of linear and macrocyclic agents. However, less retention is seen after use of macrocyclic agents.
