## 詳解附圖（OCR）
**TEACHING POINTS**

- Lynch syndrome is the most common hereditary cancer syndrome, reportedly affecting one in 400 people and accounting for approximately 3% of new CRC diagnoses.
- Lynch syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder of mismatch repair (MMR) proteins that results in poor repair of DNA replication errors. These errors preferentially accumulate in regions of the genome called microsatellites.
- Nearly all colonic malignancies in patients with Lynch syndrome exhibit MSI. However, not all microsatellite-unstable colonic malignancies are seen in patients with this condition. Up to 15% of sporadic CRCs have high MSI owing to somatic hypermethylation of the *MLH1* promoter and associated silencing of *MLH1*.
- There is an increased frequency of right-side (proximal to the splenic flexure) colon cancers, which occur in 60%–80% of individuals with Lynch syndrome compared with 30% of persons with sporadic cancers.
- The risks of individual cancers vary according to the particular MMR germline mutation and the sex of the individual.
