#交換 #2021交換 #MSK Pathologists can misinterpret the considerable cellular atypia of primary synovial osteochondromatosis as extraskeletal chondrosarcoma, if the radiologist does not communicate which one of the following imaging findings?
(A) Internal flow on ultrasound
(B) Calcifications in the mass
(C) Osseous involvement
(D) Synovial origin
(E) High signal intensity on T2-weighted MRI
??
**Ans: D**
【2021 官方詳解】Primary synovial osteochondromatosis is a benign synovial neoplastic process with slow formation of
hyaline cartilage nodules, which calcify eventually. Tenosynovial osteochondromatosis is less frequent but
can occur in the hands and feet. Calcified nodules may be visible on radiography or as dark signal on MRI.
Cartilage content produces MR T2 hyperintensity. A vascularized, fibrous pseudocapsule can produce rim
enhancement. If the lesion’s synovial origin is not relayed to the pathologist, the considerable cellular
atypia can be misinterpreted by the pathologist as extraskeletal chondrosarcoma.
