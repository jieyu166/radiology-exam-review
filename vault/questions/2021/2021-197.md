#交換 #2021交換 #ABD At postpartum uterine imaging, it is challenging to differentiate dehiscence from normal appearance of uterine incision. Which of the followings is not CORRECT?
(A) CT is better than pelvic MRI in imaging diagnosis
(B) May have free fluid in peritoneal cavity
(C) May have large bladder flap hematoma
(D) May have pleural effusion
(E) May show bowel distention
??
**Ans: A**
【2021 官方詳解】
At imaging, it is challenging to differentiate dehiscence from the normal appearance of the uterine incision. Accompanying signs such as free fluid, a large bladder flap hematoma (>5 cm), pleural effusion, and bowel distention have been suggested to support the diagnosis of uterine dehiscence. In the proper clinical setting, the presence of a large bladder flap hematoma and large pelvic hematomas should be considered highly suspicious for uterine dehiscence. The sensitivity of CT is poor for the diagnosis of uterine dehiscence. Pelvic MRI may provide a better diagnostic yield than does contrast-enhanced CT, because it can show the intact serosa and allow the differentiation of uterine rupture from dehiscence. The distinction is important because a ruptured uterus requires surgical repair.
