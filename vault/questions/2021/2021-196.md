#交換 #2021交換 #PED Ovarian torsion is a surgical emergency and challenging to make an accurate diagnosis, especially in pediatric population. Which of the followings is NOT correct?
(A) Torsion of normal adnexa can occur and is more common in the pediatric population than in adults
(B) The most commonly described finding of ovarian torsion is asymmetrical enlarged ovary
(C) Peripherally located follicles are a specific sign of torsion in adolescents
(D) Color Doppler US evaluation is reliable for confirming or excluding ovarian torsion
(E) Fluid-debris level in the ovary is a specific sign of torsion in perinatal period
??
**Ans: D**
【2021 官方詳解】
Color Doppler US evaluation is not reliable for confirming or excluding ovarian torsion. The absence of arterial and venous flow in the ovary and decreased venous flow are common Doppler US findings of ovarian torsion. However, these findings are nonspecific, because absent Doppler flow is frequently seen in nontorsed pediatric ovaries. Preserved arterial flow may be seen in cases of ovarian torsion—either secondary to lymphatic or venous obstruction producing symptoms before the later development of arterial occlusion, or owing to the dual arterial supply to the ovary from the uterine arteries. In adults, the twisted vascular pedicle has been described as a specific sign of ovarian torsion and can be seen as a round hyperechoic structure with multiple concentric hypoechoic stripes on transvaginal US images. However, this sign is less reliable in the pediatric population, as imaging in children is almost always performed by the transabdominal approach.
